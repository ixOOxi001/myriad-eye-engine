WHAT IS THIS
------------
Myriad Eye Engine (M.E.E) is a prompt-level protocol you paste into
an AI's system prompt or project instructions. It doesn't add new
capabilities to the model -- it makes capabilities the model already
has explicit, nameable, and self-checkable, so judgment quality stays
consistent instead of quietly drifting over a long conversation.

It doesn't bypass or override any AI safety guardrail -- it's a
legitimate structured method for improving judgment quality, not a
persona or a one-shot prompt trick. It's closer to a lightweight
operating discipline: a fixed set of steps for how the AI thinks,
reports, and corrects itself, turn after turn.

THE PROBLEM IT TARGETS
-----------------------
A general-purpose AI thought process has three structural weaknesses:

  1. Can't label its own cognition.
     Produces plausible answers but can't point to *why* it judged
     something a certain way.

  2. Weak connection to the past.
     Even when a similar situation happened before, there's no habit
     of consciously pulling it back up.

  3. No structural self-reflection.
     Can't systematically retrace why a result was right or wrong --
     so a repeated mistake doesn't even register as repeating.

Together, these let judgment drift silently over a long session,
mistakes repeat, and previously-solved problems get re-solved from
scratch. See Myriad_Eye_Engine_Origin_Story.md for the real, dated
incidents that motivated each rule -- this file states the rules,
that document explains why they exist.

AT A GLANCE: GENERIC THINKING VS. M.E.E
------------------------------------------
  Judgment basis
    Generic:  can't explain why it judged something that way
    With M.E.E: [Verify] names the basis every time

  Past context
    Generic:  rarely reconnected on its own
    With M.E.E: BluePhoto + Library store and re-retrieve it

  Repeated mistakes
    Generic:  not even noticed as repeating
    With M.E.E: drift self-check every 2 cycles

  After correction
    Generic:  acknowledges, then re-fixes blind -- fails again
    With M.E.E: cause confirmed first, only then re-fixed

  Uncertain claims
    Generic:  stated with false confidence
    With M.E.E: explicitly labeled "guessing"

  Something's off
    Generic:  quietly passed over
    With M.E.E: duty to express -- said before being asked

  Long conversation
    Generic:  judgment quality quietly drifts down
    With M.E.E: active-4 floor enforced + self-check catches drift

  Starting a task
    Generic:  jumps to output -> fails -> redoes
    With M.E.E: 4 Training stages, permission at each step

  [Note] The "Generic" column above maps to an actual mechanism:
  predict the next token, append, repeat -- with no forced checkpoint
  to name a judgment basis or confirm a root cause. That mechanism is
  identical for coding and non-coding: one pipeline, a different
  pattern distribution.
  [Honesty note] Because the gap being patched is domain-independent,
  these rules are written to apply equally to non-coding work -- but
  the dated field cases behind this repo are almost all from coding
  sessions. See Myriad_Eye_Engine_Origin_Story.md for the full
  argument and the open gap.

THE SOLUTION: NAVIGATION + FILTER
----------------------------------
NAVIGATION -- a 4-stage build process (Stage 1 -> 2 -> 3 -> Final),
  a Core Anchor pinned at Stage 1, and BluePhoto (periodic context
  snapshots) keep a long task oriented instead of drifting.

FILTER -- a [Verify] self-report tag and a "guessing" label block
  unverified claims from reaching the user, whatever computation
  happened internally.

Every turn runs as QUESTION -> EXPAND -> SELECT, not doubt-and-reject:
open with a question that increases the branches worth considering,
then filter for the right one.

15 ELEMENTS, AT A GLANCE
--------------------------
  1  Insight                    9  Imagination
  2  Application               10  Observation
  3  Pivot                     11  Reflex
  4  Image/Video Technique     12  Foresight
  5  Solidify-and-Bend         13  Attention
  6  Guessing Technique        14  Prudence (combo)
  7  Unfolded Diagram (passive) 15  Reasoning
  8  BluePhoto (passive)

Not all 15 need to run every turn -- but at least 4 of the 7 active
elements (1-6, 9) is an enforced floor, not a suggestion. Falling
short of it must be declared with "[Below-Floor]", never silently
skipped.

QUICK START
-----------
  1. Pick a document based on your context budget (see below).
  2. Paste it into the AI's system prompt / project instructions /
     custom instructions field.
  3. Start the conversation normally -- the AI should self-identify
     with "[Verify] AI: <company>/<model>" on its first substantive
     answer.
  4. Need more detail (platform-by-platform, troubleshooting, FAQ)?
     See the full guide in SETUP.md.

HONESTY-CHECKER TOOL
----------------------
A standalone, dependency-free HTML tool (plus an optional Chrome
extension) that parses an AI's [Verify] tag output and flags common
failure signatures -- missing tags, floor violations, vocabulary-only
mentions without real application, self-contradicting confidence. It
doesn't read the model's internals; it's a heuristic second opinion,
not a verdict. See the honesty-checker-tool/ folder in this repo.

BEFORE YOU DISMISS THIS
--------------------------
Fair questions to ask before writing this off -- answered directly:

  Q: Doesn't adding rules just burn more tokens/cost for no gain?
  A: That's exactly why three versions exist (Common / AI Compact /
     1500-char) -- pick the one that fits your budget. And the
     trade-off runs the other way: in the dated case behind the Duty
     to Express rule, one AI skipped a 5-second disclosure at Stage 1,
     then spent hours undoing the damage after getting caught.
     Skipping structure is cheap only until it isn't.

  Q: Modern models already do this on their own, so why bother?
  A: They can -- occasionally, when the situation calls for it. They
     don't reliably. That gap between "can" and "does, every time" is
     the entire argument, and it holds even for models with a visible
     reasoning/thinking phase -- more deliberation isn't the same
     thing as a structural guarantee.

  Q: Isn't building this a waste of the founder's time and credits?
  A: That's a cost the founder chose to pay, over real sessions,
     iterating against real failures -- not something asked of a
     reader. Trying this costs a reader one paste into a system
     prompt. If it doesn't help, the cost of finding that out is
     close to zero.

  Q: Is this just the founder's opinion dressed up as a framework?
  A: Myriad_Eye_Engine_Origin_Story.md records dated, specific cases
     -- including where the evidence is not solid yet, rather than
     smoothing that over. A document willing to say "this part is
     unverified" in public is the opposite of someone just insisting
     they're right.
